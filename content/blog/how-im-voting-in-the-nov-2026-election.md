Here is how I am voting on the ballot propositions in the election next month. 
I write this not necessarily as endorsements, but because the act of writing this
out makes me read all the voter material more carefully, and makes me a more 
informed voter. I'm also open to having my mind changed, if you feel differently 
on any of these — shoot me a message, and I'd be happy to get together over a 
coffee to discuss. 

Broadly, I believe in representative democracy, and believe that laws are better
passed by elected legistators, rather than directly at the ballot box. There is 
both more flexibility to repeal what turns out to be a bad idea, and more nuance
possible with debate and iterations over versions of a bill. Thus, when deciding 
how to vote on a ballot measure, I try to answer two
questions: (1) is it good policy, and (2) does it meet the bar for a ballot
measure to pass a law. The answer to *both* should be in the affirmative for me
to vote yes.

## Ballot Measures: State

- **Prop 1: Housing Bond — <span style="color:green;">YES</span>**:
Both the state assembly and senate passed this law by a pretty high margin, but
issuing new bonds needs approval by voters, so this also needs a ballot measure.
Do I think this is good policy? The low-interest mortgage is not — 
trying to fix housing affordability from the demand-side seems like a poor idea
when supply is so artificially constrained. The rest of it seems okay: subsidizing
certain housing construction with taxpayer money. I'll let the legislature's near
unanimous votes for this guide me and vote yes

- **Prop 2: Rainy Day Fund - <span style="color:green;">YES</span>**:
This proposition weakens 1979's Prop 4 (The Gann Limit on government spending),
and allows (but doesn't compel) the legislature to put more money into the rainy 
day fund. I'm for elected legislators doing their jobs, and against ballot 
initiatives constraining them, so voting yes

- **Prop 3: Tax Rate increase - <span style="color:red;">NO</span>**:
Increases marginal tax rates on high incomes by 1-3%. In general, tax slabs going up 
with time makes sense, since dollars are worth less with time due to inflation. 
I would have voted for this if this came from the legislature rather than as a
ballot initiative, or say, if it were endorsed by the governor. As it is, I don't
trust the numbers in this are set sensibly or correctly, and so safer to vote No

- **Prop 4: Public Funding of Campaigns - <span style="color:green;">YES</span>**:
Repeal's 1988's Prop 78, and allows (but does not compel) local governments to set 
up campaign financing programs. On how I feel about campaign financing — I'd prefer
leveling the playing field by limiting how much wealthy candidates can spend, rather
than by giving less wealthy candidates more money. However, I'm voting for this prop
because it seems like an okay band-aid, and because is un-constrains local governments.
